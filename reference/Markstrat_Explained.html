CORPORATE STRATEGY · IMPERIAL ESB

Markstrat in One Coffee:
The Whole Project Explained

You and your teammates have been hired as the marketing leadership of a fictional electronics company. Over the next 6 years (6 rounds), you have to beat 3 competitors and push your share price as high as possible.

01 The One-Sentence Overview

What is this assignment, really?

Markstrat is essentially a "business flight simulator" — you manage an electronics company in a virtual world. Bad decisions won't bankrupt you for real, but your score will reflect how well you played.

What's actually being assessed isn't whether you win — it's whether you can explain why.
02 What You're Selling

Two categories of fictional electronics

The professors deliberately invented product names that don't exist anywhere else — this prevents you from leaning on real-world knowledge ("I know how phones sell"). You have to learn from the data.

Mature

Sonite

The market has existed for years. 5 brands already on shelves. Consumers know the category. You start with 2 Sonite brands already in your portfolio.

Analogy: laptops / tablets / digital cameras — mature digital products.
5 core attributes: processing power, display size, design index, battery life, number of features.
Emerging

Vodite

At the start of the game, no Vodite product exists on the market yet. To enter, you first need to spend $5–10M on R&D to develop a prototype. But if the category takes off, it could be a gold mine.

Analogy: smartphones in 2007, or AR glasses / Vision Pro today.
5 core attributes: resolution, energy efficiency, carbon footprint, connectivity, number of apps.

So what are MOST, MOVE, etc.?

They're brand names — same idea as iPhone, Galaxy, ThinkPad. The naming convention:

  • 1st letter = your company code (M / R / S / T / L / N)
  • 2nd letter = product category: O = Sonite, E = Vodite
  • Remaining 4 letters are your choice

So MOST = Company M's Sonite brand, and MEGA = Company M's Vodite brand. (For us as Titans: TONE, TOPS, TOHO are Sonites; TERRA, TECHNO are Vodites.)

03 Who Your Customers Are

Consumers are split into distinct "tribes"

Each segment has different preferences and different wallets. You have to pick a few to target — try to please everyone and you'll please no one.

Sonite Market (5 segments)

Explorers
Tech enthusiasts. Want raw performance. Price-sensitive.
Shoppers
Comparison-shoppers. Want value for money.
Professionals
Business buyers. Want high quality and ease of use.
High Earners
Wealthy and image-conscious. Brand-driven.
Savers
The largest group. Pure price chasers.

Vodite Market (3 segments · Diffusion of Innovation)

Innovators
First to try anything new. High income.
Early Adopters
Opinion leaders. High influence.
Followers
The largest group. Wait for everyone else to buy first.
04 What You Decide Each Year

5 categories of decisions per round

  1. Brand Portfolio — Which brand do you upgrade? Which do you discontinue? Do you launch a new brand? Do you enter the Vodite market?
  2. R&D Projects — You commission R&D: "develop a machine with Power 80, 60-hour battery, $100 base cost". You allocate a budget; delivery is at minimum one round later.
  3. Marketing Mix (per brand) — Production volume, retail price, advertising budget, which segments to target, what message to lead with.
  4. Commercial Team — How many sales people to hire, how to split them across Specialty / Mass Merch / Online, and which brand each FTE supports.
  5. Market Research — Pay for studies: what consumers think, what competitors are doing, where the market is heading.
05 How You Win

SPI is the final scoreboard

Share Price Index (SPI)

SPI combines the 4 factors below. Profitable + high volume + growing + investing in R&D → high SPI → you win.

Net Profit
How much you earned
Market Share
How much of the market you own
Revenue Growth
Whether revenue is sustainably growing
R&D Projects
How much useful R&D you've completed

07 The Real Prize

The Final Report is worth 70%

Maximum 2,000 words, double-spaced, font size 12. Cover page, table of contents, appendices and references do not count toward the word limit. Marking: structure & readability 40%, quality of argument 60%.

"Performance does not matter; understanding matters."
— from the course brief

Even if your SPI is mediocre, you can still earn a high mark if you can clearly articulate why you made each decision, where you misjudged, and why. Conversely, winning but failing to explain still loses marks.

Frameworks recommended for the report

BCG Matrix · Porter's Five Forces · First-Mover Advantage · SWOT · STP (Segmentation–Targeting–Positioning) · Diffusion of Innovation · Cash Flow Risk · Experience Curve · Scenario Planning (Schoemaker 1995)

The single most important habit

After every round, immediately log 3 things: ① what we decided and why; ② which data / research supported that decision; ③ what we predicted would happen. Then once next round's results land, add: what actually happened, and where the gap was. Those gaps are gold for the report.

08 Practical Tips

5 things to tell the team

1. Don't go wild in Round 1
Use the 4 free studies first (Consumer Survey, Consumer Panel, Distribution Panel, Market Forecast) to understand the market. Read before you act. Restraint in Round 1 beats aggression in Round 1.
2. The Vodite question is the biggest strategic call
First-mover advantage is real, but before any Vodite brand exists there's no perceptual map yet — you'd be flying blind. Enter early = grab innovators and shape the category. Enter late = lower risk but share may already be locked. This decision deserves its own team meeting.
3. Aim 2–3 periods ahead of where consumers are now
Consumer "ideal points" drift slowly over time. R&D takes at least one full period to complete. If you target today's ideal point, by the time your product ships, the market has moved on. Aim where the puck is going, not where it is.
4. Don't ignore "cost reduction" R&D
Once cumulative production is high enough, you can commission a same-spec project with a lower base cost — this dramatically improves margins. Most teams forget this lever exists.
5. Draft the report as you go
Don't wait until all 6 rounds finish to look back — you'll forget the details. Spend 30 minutes after each round capturing decisions and reflections. At the end, integration is much faster than starting from scratch.
