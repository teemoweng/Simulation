PART 03 · WHAT WORKED — AND WHY

4 Decisions That Delivered

✓Overtook Marvels: +263 SPI swing in one period
P2 we were 162 behind. P3 we are 101 ahead. Net turnaround: 263 SPI.
Why it happened: Marvels bet on MOVE's $575 upgrade targeting Professionals — a valid move but one that raised their ASP while reducing volume traction. Simultaneously, our TOHO launch opened a new revenue stream, our commercial team expansion (to 160 FTE) widened distribution, and TOPS CAM grew +20%. Marvels' MOST also shed value share (−4pp). We didn't beat Marvels in a single area — we outperformed them on aggregate across four areas simultaneously.
✓POTONECRP3 completed for just $100K — base cost $65
Same TONE specs, base cost drops from $74 → $65 (−12%). Zero additional R&D needed.
Why it matters: Switching TONE from POTONE to POTONECRP3 in Round 4 costs nothing and immediately reduces cost on every unit. With cumulative production of ~575K, each $1 of cost reduction saves ~$0.5M annually at current volumes. The POTONECRP3 switch also increases our price-war resilience — ROCK at $200 is pressuring TONE's Savers base, and a lower cost floor means we can respond without destroying margin.
✓TOHO first-period launch: 60K sold, new revenue stream open
Revenue $8.8M, 4.9% volume share, 38%/43%/32% channel coverage — all in the first period.
Why it worked: The 160-FTE commercial team gave TOHO instant distribution muscle. A new brand normally struggles with coverage; TOHO's 38% Specialty and 43% Mass Merch first-period coverage matches what established brands took 2–3 rounds to build. The downside is supply — TOHO is selling every unit we make, which means the production floor has been the binding constraint, not demand.
✓TOPS CAM hit $24.3M (+20%) — the company's profit engine is growing
TOPS now generates 66% of company contribution. Value share #1 in industry at 16%.
Why it worked: Experience curve (unit cost $127 → $119) combined with stable $500 pricing expanded margin per unit, while volume held at 142K. The −42% advertising cut in P2 did cause volume share to slip −1.2pp, but the salesforce upgrade and price positioning more than compensated in contribution terms. Round 4 needs to restore TOPS advertising — MOVE's upgrade now makes Professionals more contested.
PART 04 · WHAT WENT WRONG — AND WHY

4 Issues Round 4 Must Fix

!Issue 1: TOHO supply failure (2nd period in a row)
Planned 50K, system auto-raised to 60K, still sold out. Lost sales warning issued again.
Root cause: We continue to under-estimate Shoppers demand. The segment is ~265K in P3. TOHO's positioning (features 7, design 8 — closest to Shoppers' ideal) should be capturing 20%+ of the segment = ~53K+ from Shoppers alone, plus Savers overlap. Our 60K ceiling was never going to be enough.

Round 4 fix: Plan TOHO at 120K. The ±20% ceiling puts actual range at 96–144K. At 120K production, experience curve will push unit cost from $124 → ~$105, CAM should turn positive for the first time.
!Issue 2: TONE and TOPS both over-produced (first inventory ever)
TONE: planned 240K, auto-cut to 192K, sold 186K → 6K inventory. TOPS: planned 180K, auto-cut to 144K, sold 142K → 2K inventory.
Root cause: We extrapolated last period's demand trajectory without accounting for market saturation signals. Sonite market grew +19% (P2→P3) but both TONE and TOPS lost share — competitors SOLO (+49K) and MOVE/ROLL absorbed more of the new demand. We over-planned based on market growth without adjusting for competitive share pressure.

Round 4 fix: TONE plan ~210K, TOPS plan ~155–165K. Also note: ROCK's price drop to $200 will likely steal some TONE Savers volume in P4 — plan conservatively.
!Issue 3: Value share fell 4pp (28% → 24%) despite revenue growth
Company volume share held at 30%, but value share dropped from 28% to 24%.
Root cause: TOHO's low ASP ($146 average selling price vs $223 retail) drags company value share down. TOHO sold $8.8M on 60K units — a low-ASP launch. TONE at $141 ASP also pulls the mix lower. TOPS at $310 ASP is the only value-driver. As TOHO scales and matures, this should recover — but short-term it's a mix signal to watch.

Watch for: If value share keeps falling in P4, it signals we're growing in low-margin segments while losing high-margin ground to MOVE and SOLO.
!Issue 4: Missed Vodite's entire first period — 0% of a $70M market
Snipers (69%) and Rocket (31%) split the $70M Vodite market between them. We weren't there.
Root cause: R&D was launched in P3 but isn't complete yet — Vodite projects typically require 2 periods. The decision to invest $8.6M in Vodite R&D in P3 was correct, but it means we can't launch until P4 at the earliest. The opportunity cost is significant: missing P3 Vodite = missing Innovators segment SPI.

Round 4 imperative: If Vodite R&D shows as complete in the P4 R&D report, launch immediately. Target Early Adopters (not Innovators — Snipers has already claimed them). Do not wait for P5.

PART 07 · THE VODITE IMPERATIVE

Why Vodite Is the Highest-Priority Decision in Round 4

The numbers: Vodite generated $70M in retail sales in its first period. Snipers captured $48M of that. Their SPI jumped +552. We got $0 and our SPI grew only +115.

The projection: Vodite market will grow faster than Sonites in every future period (Rogers diffusion — Early Adopters and Early Majority are much larger than Innovators). The SPI gap between Vodite-present and Vodite-absent firms will compound each period.

The decision rule: If Vodite R&D shows "Available: Period 4" in the P4 R&D report — launch the brand immediately. Budget ~$2M for Vodite launch marketing. Do not delay to P5 to "save budget" — that's the same mistake we made going into P3.

Target segment: Early Adopters (not Innovators — Snipers owns that). Early Adopters are price-conscious but technology-curious. Position our Vodite brand at a slightly lower price than SESENEW with advertising targeting Early Adopters directly.

PART 08 · ROUND 4 PLAYBOOK

7 Priorities for the Team

  1. Launch Vodite brand (if R&D complete) — #1 priority ⭐
    Check R&D Report first. If POTOVO (or equivalent Vodite platform) shows "Available: P4", launch immediately. Production ~40–60K, price just below SESENEW, advertising focused on Early Adopters. Budget ~$2M for launch. This is the highest-SPI move available to us.
  2. TOHO production: plan 120K — no more stockouts
    Two consecutive sell-outs = validated demand signal. Shoppers segment is ~265K. Plan 120K (ceiling 144K). Cost will drop from $124 → ~$105 at that volume. CAM should turn positive. The lost sales cost from underproduction far exceeds the inventory carrying cost of modest overproduction.
  3. Switch TONE to POTONECRP3 platform (free, immediate)
    In Brand Portfolio decisions, change TONE's base project from POTONE to POTONECRP3. Same specs, base cost drops $74 → $65. This costs nothing in R&D and saves ~$9/unit × 186K units = ~$1.7M additional contribution per period. Also builds price-war resilience against ROCK at $200.
  4. TONE and TOPS production: dial back to actual demand
    TONE: plan 210K (not 240K). TOPS: plan 155–165K (not 180K). With ROCK at $200, TONE demand may soften further in P4 — don't over-commit. Inventory costs money and signals poor planning in the BCG analysis.
  5. Restore TOPS advertising — Professionals are under attack
    TOPS advertising was cut −42% in P2, and TOPS value share fell −1.6pp this period. MOVE (Power 84, Battery 72) is now the most technically capable Professionals product. Restore TOPS media budget to ~$1.5M and increase research allocation to 15% (target Professionals' MDS position).
  6. Initiate TOPS R&D upgrade — Power and Battery for Professionals
    MOVE's upgrade makes TOPS' current specs (Power 75, Battery 40) look dated to Professionals. Start a POTOPS2 project targeting Power 80+, Battery 60+. Budget $1.5–2M. Will be available P5 — which is when the Professionals battle will be decisive.
  7. Monitor ROCK's price war impact on TONE — don't panic-cut
    ROCK at $200 is a threat, but Rocket may not sustain it (their margin at $200 and $74 cost is thin). Wait for P4 Savers share data before cutting TONE price. Hold $225 for now — POTONECRP3 already gives us cost room. A reflexive price cut could trigger a race to the bottom that hurts everyone.
Round 4 theme: "Vodite entry + TOHO scale + Sonite defense." We have ~$11.3M budget (after loan repayment). The Snipers gap of 177 SPI is closable — but only if we enter Vodite immediately. Every period we're absent from Vodite is a period where Snipers widens the gap. TOHO at 120K will turn CAM-positive and open the Shoppers segment properly. TONE and TOPS need defensive tweaks, not transformations.
