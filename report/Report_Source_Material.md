## Framework 3 · Diffusion of Innovation (Rogers 1962)

> **Why this framework:** Vodite is a new category; its growth follows S-curve diffusion. Our timing and segment targeting maps directly onto Innovators → Adopters → Followers.

### Vodite market trajectory

| Period | Industry retail (M$) | Active brands | Our position | Predicted | Actual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2 | 0 | 0 | Outside | — | — |
| P3 | 70 | 1 (SESENEW) | Outside (R&D investment $7M for PEINNO) | TERRA launch in P4 will catch P3 first-mover | — |
| P4 | 106 | 4 (SESENEW, TERRA, READ, MEME) | **TERRA 47.6% value share · #1 by close margin** | Splitting the market with Snipers | Confirmed — Marvels/Rocket lock-out at 2-3% |
| P5 | ~144 | 5 (+ TECHNO) | TERRA 16% + TECHNO 12% = **28%** | Maintain 45-52% Vodite share total | **Failed** — SESENEW pulled to 58%; Marvels' MEME revived to 9% |
| P6 | ~283 | 5 | TECHNO 16% + TERRA 9% = **25%** | hold ~28% | Bled further; **SESENEW 54% (dominant), MEME 20%** — the late entrants did NOT stay locked out |

> **Diffusion reversal:** the P4 "duopoly lock-out" thesis did not hold. As the Vodite category moved from the Innovator wave into the larger Adopter/Follower waves (industry retail ~$106M → ~$283M over P4→P6), the segments that grew were exactly the ones the *late* entrants targeted. Marvels' MEME (low-spec, low-cost $202, priced $660) rode the price-sensitive Follower wave from 2% (P4) to 20% (P6). Our two premium-priced brands ($710) were positioned for the *early* part of the curve and got left behind as the mass of the market shifted down-market and down-price.

### Adoption-curve segment timing

| Segment | Defining timing | Our action |
|---|---|---|
| Innovators | P3-P5 wave | TERRA launched in P4 (1 period after Snipers); captured 47.6% — within reach of #1 |
| Early Adopters | P5-P6 wave | TERRA shifts allocation to Adopters in P5 (45% vs 30%) |
| Followers | P6-P9 wave | TECHNO probes early (P5) — Adith's diversification bet |

### 📝 Report bullet candidates
- "Vodite Period 4 outcome — Titans + Snipers locking out 96% of the value market — is a clean illustration of Rogers' diffusion model: the first one or two entrants in a high-growth category establish position before late entrants (Marvels MEME, Rocket READ) can credibly compete for Innovators. Our 1-period lag versus Snipers cost us EBT leadership ($8.2M vs $11.3M Vodite EBT) but not strategic position."
- "TECHNO in P5 is a forward bet: targeting the Followers segment ahead of full segment growth. Diffusion models predict Followers eventually become the largest Vodite segment (cf. Markstrat 5-year forecast), so early brand-building here builds barriers before competitors arrive."
- **(P6 reversal — the strongest Diffusion bullet)** "Our Period-4 reading of Rogers' model — that the first two entrants lock out the category — proved *half right*. First-movers were rewarded (Snipers' SESENEW, in from P3, ended at 54% share), but the 'lock-out' of late entrants did not hold once diffusion moved past the Innovators. By P6 the late entrant Marvels (MEME) had reached 20%, overtaking both of our brands. The corrected lesson: first-mover advantage in a diffusing category protects you only for the segment you entered on. The category's centre of gravity *moves down the adoption curve* — and a Follower-priced product (MEME at $660, base cost $202) captured the larger, later wave that our two premium $710 brands were mis-positioned for. We had the right brand *concept* (TECHNO for Followers) but starved it of the price and cost position the Follower wave actually rewards."

## Framework 5 · Scenario Planning (Schoemaker 1995)

> **Why this framework:** Required reading. Schoemaker argues good strategy ≠ accurate forecast; it = preparation for multiple plausible futures. Our Vodite entry decision is a clean Scenario Planning case.

### Vodite entry decision (P2 → P3 deliberation)

**Uncertainty:** Would Vodite become a real category, or remain niche?

**Two scenarios we faced:**

| Scenario | Trigger | Our Response |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Vodite becomes mainstream** | Snipers' early entry succeeds → market accelerates | Enter quickly to share the duopoly (high upside, $7M R&D investment is recoverable in 1-2 periods) |
| **B. Vodite stays niche** | First-mover struggles → market never reaches scale | Stay out, focus on Sonites cost-leadership |

**Decision:** Enter (PEINNO R&D in P3, TERRA launch in P4). This was a Scenario A bet — but defensible under Schoemaker's framework because Scenario A's upside ($10M+ CAM) materially outweighs Scenario B's modest cost-saving from staying out.

### Outcome variance
- **Scenario A confirmed:** Vodite industry retail went 0 → $70M → $106M in two periods. By P4, Vodite represents 27% of Titans revenue and 16% of net contribution.
- **Late entrants got locked out:** Marvels and Rocket entered P4 but captured only 2-3% share each — confirming the Scenario Planning insight that *timing windows close*.

### 📝 Report bullet candidates
- "The Vodite entry decision exemplifies Schoemaker's (1995) scenario approach: we did not forecast Vodite would reach $106M industry retail by Period 4 with 95% certainty. Rather, we identified two plausible scenarios (mainstream vs niche) and chose the one whose upside materially exceeded its downside cost. The actual outcome (Scenario A confirmed) validated the framework, not because we predicted accurately, but because we *prepared* for the higher-leverage outcome."
- "A more cautious team (e.g. Marvels, Rocket) waited for the Vodite category to be 'proven' before entering — and by then the timing window had closed. This illustrates Schoemaker's central thesis: in conditions of strategic uncertainty, *option-creation under multiple scenarios* dominates *waiting for certainty*."
- **(P5–P6 — the scenario we DIDN'T plan for)** "Schoemaker's framework cuts both ways. We planned well for the *entry* uncertainty (will Vodite be a category?) but failed to scenario-plan the *competitive-response* uncertainty (what happens when the duopoly becomes a five-way fight?). Our P5 decisions implicitly bet on a single scenario — 'we keep ~48% Vodite share' — and committed production to it (72K + 80K units across two brands). When the alternative scenario materialised (share halving as Marvels/Rocket re-entered and Snipers pulled away), we had no contingency: the over-committed inventory cost us $1.9M in holding charges and forced a panic price-cut in P6. The lesson echoes Schoemaker exactly — we stopped generating scenarios once the *first* uncertainty resolved in our favour, and a single-point forecast crept back in."

## 📋 Round-by-Round Decision Log (for Appendix or quick reference)

### Round 1 (P1 decision · submitted May 1)
**Decision summary:** Maintain TONE + TOPS as Sonites baseline. No R&D. Standard ad spend.
**Predicted:** Hold rank 2-3.
**Actual:** Rank #2, SPI 1,165 (behind Rocket by 63).
**Gap / lesson:** Conservative; we didn't lose ground but didn't gain.

### Round 2 (P2 decision · submitted May 8)
**Decision summary:** Initiated POSHOP R&D ($1.44M) for future Vodite play. Cut advertising 26% to fund R&D. Doubled commercial team.
**Predicted:** Maintain rank with stronger fundamentals.
**Actual:** Rank #2 but **Marvels overtook us by 162 SPI**. TONE became industry volume #1.
**Gap / lesson:** Ad cut too aggressive given competitive intensity; Marvels exploited the gap.

### Round 3 (P3 decision · submitted May 12)
**Decision summary:** Launched TOHO (Sonites #3, Shoppers). Major Vodite R&D investment ($8.7M for PEINNO). Took $5.66M loan.
**Predicted:** Rank #2, prepare for Vodite entry.
**Actual:** Rank #2, SPI 1,539. **Snipers SESENEW launched Vodite first and leapt +552 SPI**.
**Gap / lesson:** Our P2 R&D timing meant we entered Vodite one period after Snipers — costly but not fatal. POSHOP R&D paid off (TOHO worked).

### Round 4 (P4 decision · submitted May 15)
**Decision summary:** Launched TERRA (Vodite, PEINNO-based). Switched TONE to POTONECRP3 (cost reduction). All five marketing mixes optimised.
**Predicted:** Close gap with Snipers, TOHO profitable.
**Actual:** **Biggest SPI gain in our 4 rounds (+331)**. TERRA captured 47.6% Vodite. TOHO swung +$4.5M (loss → profit). Gap to Snipers narrowed from 177 to 42.
**Gap / lesson:** Strategy worked, but execution left money on the table — Sonites R&D = $0 (should have done POTOPS reduction), TOHO production planned at 100K (should have been 120K), ads flat across brands (should have followed experiment ROI).

### Round 5 (P5 decision · submitted May 20)
**Decision summary — what was ACTUALLY submitted (differs sharply from the Decision Guide):**
- **R&D: $0** — none of the three planned projects (POTOPS cost-reduction, POTOPSV2, POSHOPV2) were funded. The guide called for $2,800K. ⚠️ **biggest deviation**
- Advertising raised to ~$8.4M media total, but TOPS got only ~$1.45M (guide: $1.7M); the bulk went to the two Vodite brands (~$2.25M each)
- TONE priced $220 (guide: $215), TOHO $235, TOPS $499, TERRA $740, TECHNO $720
- **Vodite over-production:** TECHNO planned 90K / TERRA planned 100K — far above what the new/crowded market absorbed
- Commercial team scaled hard to ~$7.7M cost (≈ doubled vs P4)
- TECHNO launched (Adith, PENT-based, Vodite Followers)

**Predicted (Decision Guide):** SPI 1,950–2,100 · Revenue $140–155M · EBT $55–65M · TOHO CAM $6–8M, TOPS CAM $28–32M, TERRA CAM $14–18M, TECHNO CAM $2–4M

**Actual:** SPI **1,779 (#2, DOWN 91)** · Revenue **$118.8M** (flat) · EBT **$43.1M** (down) · TOHO CAM **$5.96M**, TOPS CAM **$26.6M**, TERRA CAM **$2.33M**, TECHNO CAM **−$0.66M** · Vodite share halved to 28% · **$1.93M inventory-holding cost** (highest of all four firms)

**Gap / lesson:** Almost every miss traces to two deviations from plan — (1) R&D was zeroed, so no product or cost improvement landed; (2) Vodites were over-produced into a market that had quietly become a 5-way fight. Revenue stalled while costs (commercial team + ads + inventory) surged, so EBT *fell* in a period we'd forecast a record. First SPI decline of the game.

### Round 6 (P6 decision · submitted May 22) — FINAL ROUND
**Decision summary — what was ACTUALLY submitted:**
- **R&D: $0 again** — TOPS base cost ended at $193 vs Snipers' SOLO $174
- **Across-the-board price cuts:** TONE $220→$200, TOHO $235→$210, TOPS $499→$480, TERRA $740→$710 — partly to clear the P5 Vodite inventory glut
- Vodite production slashed to 20K each (TECHNO/TERRA) to liquidate ~47K of leftover inventory per brand
- Advertising held high (~$8.9M media); commercial team held at ~$7.9M

**Predicted (implicit):** stabilise, defend #2, possibly close on Snipers

**Actual:** SPI **1,707 — DROPPED to #3** (Marvels overtook us, 1,740) · Revenue **$132.9M** · EBT **$47.2M** (recovered vs P5 but only ≈ P4 level) · all five brands lost value share · Vodite share down to 25% · Snipers ran away to SPI **2,667** with $108.9M net contribution (2.3× ours)

**Gap / lesson:** EBT recovered (inventory cleared, prices moved volume) but contribution had *flat-lined* for two periods while every competitor grew — so SPI, a relative/forward-looking measure, kept falling. The price cuts protected volume at the expense of the premium positioning that had been our edge. Finished 3rd of 4: solid absolute fundamentals (cumulative EBT $236.9M), but out-compounded in the final third.

## 🪙 Highest-Value Variances (Report Gold)

**Positive variances (P1–P4):**
1. **TOHO swing −$1.95M → +$2.57M in one period (P3 → P4):** Production scale + experience curve. (Largest single-brand swing in the early game.)
2. **Snipers' Vodite first-mover (P3 SESENEW = +552 SPI):** Biggest competitor single-round move; informed our P4 TERRA timing.
3. **Our P4 +331 SPI (TERRA launch + TOHO turn):** Biggest single-round gain; closed the gap to Snipers to 42.

**Negative variances (P5–P6) — the most valuable material for a "60% argument-quality" report:**
4. **The R&D that was planned but never funded (P5 & P6 = $0):** Predicted $2.7M/yr return from POTOPS cost-reduction; actual = a *widening* $19/unit cost gap vs Snipers' SOLO ($193 vs $174 by P6). The cleanest "we knew the right lever and didn't pull it" story in the whole game.
5. **TECHNO predicted +$2–4M CAM, actual −$0.66M:** Treating a Question Mark like a Star — over-produced 72K for a brand that sold 25K. $1.0M inventory cost on a single first-period brand.
6. **Vodite share 48% → 25% in two periods:** The "duopoly lock-out" thesis (P4) inverted — late entrant Marvels/MEME went 2%→20%, overtaking both our brands. Diffusion's centre of gravity moved down-curve and down-price; our two premium $710 brands were left behind.
7. **SPI fell two periods straight (1,870 → 1,779 → 1,707) on RISING revenue and EBT:** The counter-intuitive crown jewel — absolute performance held, but *relative/forward-looking* value fell because contribution flat-lined while every rival grew. Illustrates why SPI ≠ profit.
8. **The over-extension trade (4 → 5 brands):** We diluted one budget across five sub-scale brands; Snipers concentrated three (two focused Sonites + one dominant Vodite) and out-earned us 2.3× on net contribution. BCG's "concentrate behind Stars" lesson, learned the hard way.

> The strongest report combines **one positive arc** (Vodite entry timing + TOHO turnaround, P1–P4) with **the negative arc** (the final-third plateau: R&D starvation + over-extension + the SPI-vs-profit paradox, P5–P6). The *variance between plan and execution* — we wrote the right Round-5 plan and then didn't follow it — is itself the report's sharpest insight: strategy formulation and strategy *execution* are different disciplines, and the simulation graded the second one.
