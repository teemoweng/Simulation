Our absolute results were fine — revenue grew double-digits and EBT ended marginally up. But contribution flat-lined, and SPI is a relative, forward-looking measure: a stock price reflects momentum versus rivals, not just this period's profit. While we held steady, Snipers' net contribution went $77M → $109M and Marvels' $42M → $57M. Standing still in a race where everyone else sprints is, mechanically, falling behind.

Why this is the most important lesson of the game

A team optimising for this-period profit would have called Periods 5–6 a success (EBT recovered, every brand profitable in P6). The simulation didn't, because SPI rewards compounding trajectory. The strategic takeaway — and the spine of our final report — is that defending a position requires the same investment intensity that built it. We switched from building to harvesting one round too early.

Portfolio takeaway

Five brands, none above 16% in its own market. We spread one budget across five sub-scale offerings. Snipers ran three brands — two focused Sonites plus the dominant Vodite (SESENEW, 54%) — and earned 2.3× our net contribution. Concentration beat diversification.

POST-MORTEM

What Won, What Lost

✓ What we got right (P1–P4)

  • Vodite entry timing. Followed Snipers by one period with TERRA; captured 47.6% at launch. A textbook scenario-planning bet.
  • TOHO turnaround. A Question Mark driven to a profitable Star via production scale + experience curve (−$1.95M → +$2.57M).
  • POTONECRP3 cost-reduction. $100K of R&D cut TONE's unit cost 27% and doubled its CAM — proof we understood the highest-ROI lever.
  • Segmentation discipline. Non-overlapping Sonite targets (Savers / Shoppers / Pros) avoided cannibalisation through P4.

✗ What cost us the podium (P5–P6)

  • R&D went to $0 for two periods. The planned POTOPS cost-reduction was never funded; we ended with a $19/unit cost gap vs Snipers' SOLO.
  • Over-extension into a 5th brand. A second Vodite brand split our own franchise; combined Vodite share fell 48% → 25%.
  • Over-production. ~95K unsold Vodite units in P5 → $1.9M inventory cost, forcing P6 price cuts.
  • Harvested too early. Cut prices and coasted on the brand equity we'd built, instead of re-investing to defend #2.

FOR THE FINAL REPORT

The Frameworks This Season Hands Us

Experience CurvePorterThe R&D we diagnosed but didn't fund

We wrote the highest-ROI move in the game (POTOPS cost-reduction, ~2-month payback) and then didn't execute it for two periods, ending with a structural $19/unit cost disadvantage. Formulation ≠ execution — and the simulation graded execution.

BCGSTPConcentration vs over-extension

Five sub-scale brands lost to three focused ones. BCG prescribes concentrating investment behind Stars; we diluted it. The second Vodite brand cannibalised the first rather than expanding the pie.

DiffusionFirst-MoverThe lock-out that wasn't

Our P4 "duopoly lock-out" read inverted: as diffusion moved from Innovators to the larger Follower wave, late entrant Marvels (MEME, $660) went 2% → 20%, overtaking our two premium $710 brands. First-mover advantage protects only the segment you entered on.

Scenario PlanningThe scenario we stopped generating

We scenario-planned the entry question well, then reverted to a single-point forecast ("we keep 48% Vodite share") and committed production to it. When the competitive-response scenario hit, we had no contingency. Schoemaker (1995), exactly: keep generating scenarios even after the first uncertainty resolves in your favour.
