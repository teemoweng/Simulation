PART 03 · WHAT WORKED — AND WHY

The 5 Decisions That Got Us to #2

✓Dual-brand differentiated positioning — fully validated
TONE captured 24.0% share in Savers and 21.8% in Shoppers; TOPS took 24.4% in Pros and 24.9% in High Earners. Zero brand cannibalization.
Why it worked: All 4 firms have identical 8 brands at the physical level — the only levers are advertising focus + channel alignment + price differentiation. We put 100% of advertising into the home segments (Savers/Shoppers for TONE, Pros/HE for TOPS), aligned salespeople by shopping habits, and our purchase intentions concentrated exactly where we wanted.
✓Channel-segment alignment — TONE → Mass, TOPS → Specialty
Of TONE's 118K units, 64K (54%) sold through Mass Merchandisers; of TOPS' 98K, 63K (64%) through Specialty Stores.
Why it worked: 60% of Savers shop at Mass Merch, 60% of Pros shop at Specialty. We shifted the salesforce from a flat 10/10/10 to 10/15/5 (TONE-heavy on Mass) + 17/8/5 (TOPS-heavy on Specialty), perfectly matching where each segment actually buys.
✓TOPS price hike +4% gained share AND margin
$500 → $520, but Volume Share +0.5pp, Value Share +1.3pp, contribution +71%.
Why it worked: Pros and HE treat price as a quality signal — higher price = more "professional" perception. Semantic Scales shows HE's ideal Price = 5.9 (the highest, on a 1–7 scale). Combined with low price elasticity at the high end. Implication: TOPS can raise price further in Round 2.
✓Bought every market research study — ammunition for Round 2
Spent $437K on 11 studies, including Semantic Scales, MDS, both Experiments.
Why it pays off: Hidden investment with no Round 1 ROI but decisive for Round 2. MDS revealed Shoppers is the largest "no-man's land" in the market (17+ units from any brand). Experiment data tells us precisely "+20% advertising loses $60K, +30 salespeople gains $1.7M." These two findings drive every Round 2 move.
✓Free dividend from the experience curve
Cumulative production doubled → unit cost dropped ~15%. TONE $70 → $59, TOPS $169 → $143.
Why it worked: Markstrat's built-in experience curve — every doubling of cumulative output cuts unit cost by ~15%. Even with no action, costs keep falling. Implication: maintaining high volume is more cost-effective than chasing cost-reduction R&D.
PART 04 · WHAT WE DIDN'T DO WELL — AND WHY

3 Issues Round 2 Must Fix

!Issue 1: Conservative production → both brands sold out and lost sales
TONE planned 110K, sold 118K (auto-adjusted +7%); TOPS planned 84K, sold 98K (auto-adjusted +15.5%, nearly hit the +20% cap).
Root cause: Our forecast model was too cautious — only factored in market growth (+16.5%) and share-shift effects, but didn't account for "competitors are also conservative, so our relative share could rise more."

Lost revenue: TOPS unit margin ~$178. If we'd planned 100K, the auto-adjusted ceiling would have been 120K, potentially earning an extra $1.5M+.

Round 2 fix: TONE plan 145K, TOPS plan 130K. Market is still growing 16.5% and we have share momentum — anything less leaves money on the table.
!Issue 2: Advertising research ratio too high (22–27%) — wasted spend
We spent $1.3M on advertising research (24% of $5.3M total), but the Experiment shows +20% advertising would actually lose $60–130K.
Root cause: We wanted to amplify the persuasive power of new positioning, but in fact:
① We didn't set any Perceptual Objectives (left blank)
② All 8 brands have identical physical specs — advertising can't change perception of physical attributes
③ Awareness was already 53–81% — diminishing marginal returns

Estimated waste: Cutting research from 24% to 8% would have saved ~$0.85M.

Round 2 fix: Drop research to 8–12% (awareness-maintenance level). Redirect savings to R&D.
!Issue 3: Marvels stole some Savers share — TONE value share down
MOST cut price $250 → $215 (−14%), doubled salesforce to 60 FTE, and grabbed 32.9% in Savers (TONE only 24%).
Root cause: Marvels chose a "low-end dominance" play — aggressive price cut + sales investment. We only nudged price −2% ($250 → $245) and held headcount at 60 total. Savers are extremely price-sensitive (ideal Price = 2.2, lowest on the 1–7 scale), so a $215 vs $245 gap was enough to shift purchase intent.

But: Our TOPS performance crushed everyone (contribution +71%), so total contribution still grew +50%. The "small loss" was buried under the "big win."

Round 2 fix: Don't enter a full-blown price war (margin destruction). Drop TONE to $235 + initiate cost-reduction R&D (push base cost from $59 toward $45) — keeps higher margin even at $235.
